An industrial plant is described by many kinds of documents: piping and instrumentation diagrams (P&IDs) show how the items are connected, vendor datasheets give each device's specification. Engineering questions often need both. Our two earlier projects covered the two sides of the same documentation: one builds a plant knowledge graph from multi-sheet drawings and answers over it with a governed Cypher-then-agent cascade, the other turns a single device's datasheet into an ISO 15926 / QUDT graph that keeps conditions and cross-references.

This work joins the two. For a seeded four-sheet example plant in which every one of the 24 tagged physical items has a vendor datasheet, we load the plant layer and the datasheet graphs of seven devices into one Neo4j database and link them by tag. We compare three methods: the cascade extended with the datasheet layer and a new datasheet primitive (M1), a router over the two existing systems (M2), and a graph-free baseline (M3). We built 60 questions in four families (drawing only, datasheet only, combined, unanswerable) with programmatically assembled reference answers, and froze a 40-question test set.

On the test set the router with GLM-5.3 Flash was best (31 of 40 correct), the graph-free baseline answered 30 and the merged-graph method M1 25 correctly; with GPT-6 Luna the differences were not significant. No method hallucinated, and all of them recognised the unanswerable questions. The differences appear on datasheet and combined questions, where M1's single-query answers are often incomplete. M1 was the most expensive per question and M3 the cheapest. The results hold for one small synthetic plant with model-written references.

Keywords: knowledge graph, GraphRAG, large language models, P&ID, technical datasheets, question answering, industrial documentation.
