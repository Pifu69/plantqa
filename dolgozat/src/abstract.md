An industrial plant is described by thousands of pages of heterogeneous documentation. Piping and instrumentation diagrams (P&IDs) show how equipment is connected, and vendor datasheets give each device's specification. Engineers regularly need to answer questions that span these documents. Large language models (LLMs) are an obvious candidate for this, but plant documentation is hard for them. Much of the relevant information is not contained in any single element. It lives in the context: in a table header, in a footnote that restricts a value, or in an off-page connector that continues a pipe on another drawing sheet. Graph-based retrieval-augmented generation (GraphRAG) promises to make this structure explicit, but only if the graph keeps that context.

Our joint work studies this problem from two complementary sides of the same documentation.

The drawing side looks at GraphRAG over P&IDs at plant scale. Published approaches turn a single diagram sheet into a graph and give the whole graph to the model. We examine whether this strategy still holds when the corpus is a whole plant region spanning many interconnected sheets. To make this measurable, we build a multi-sheet benchmark with cross-sheet ground truth. It is built from synthetically generated, reproducible plant graphs split into sheets, where every cut connection becomes an off-page connector pair with a known answer. We also evaluate cross-sheet entity resolution: linking connectors between sheets and recognising one device as the same entity when it is drawn on several sheets.

The datasheet side turns the vendor datasheet of a single device (a pump, a valve or a sensor alike) into a queryable knowledge graph in four stages. First, existing extraction methods are compared on converting datasheet pages into a common, equipment-independent record format: parameter, value, unit, source location, applicability conditions and cross-references. The compared methods are direct multimodal LLM prompting, Azure Document Intelligence, and Docling with LLM post-processing. The comparison also covers content that text-based tools tend to lose, such as dimension drawings, sectional views and performance charts. Second, the extracted records are mapped to established vocabularies: QUDT for quantities and units, and industrial reference data libraries for equipment properties. Third, a graph is built from this representation in which conditions and cross-references remain explicit elements instead of being flattened into simple triples. This matters because a temperature limit that holds only for one body material becomes wrong, not merely incomplete, once its condition is dropped. Finally, alternative graph-building methods are compared on representative engineering questions, including multi-hop questions that require following a cross-reference or respecting a condition.

Together, the two lines of work examine one question: how structure and context can be preserved when industrial documentation is turned into a graph, and how retrieval strategies should be evaluated when the answer depends on that context. Both use purpose-built, controlled ground truth instead of single-page examples. Linking the per-device datasheet graphs to the equipment on the drawings is a natural extension that would give a single queryable model of the plant.

In this thesis we take that step: we link the drawing graph and the datasheet graphs of seven devices into one knowledge graph by tag, and compare three question-answering methods on a 100-sheet and a 1000-sheet synthetic plant not used during tuning. A router that calls the two systems separately did best with GLM-5.3 Flash (51 and 48 of 60 correct), and the method over the merged graph was not significantly behind it. The graph-free baseline gave fewer correct answers, and the 1000-sheet drawing no longer fits into the models' context, while the per-question cost of the graph-based methods at most doubled on the ten times larger drawing.

Keywords: knowledge graph, GraphRAG, large language models, P&ID, technical datasheets, question answering, industrial documentation.
